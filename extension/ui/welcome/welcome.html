Tise learns from your browsing. It never leaves your device.

Read this before turning it on. Tise has stored nothing so far, and will not until you press the button at the bottom of this page.

Collecting.

What it does

It counts what you already do, and tells you the pattern

Every time you go to a page, Tise reduces the address to a bare site name, sorts that into one of fifteen topics, and writes down that one line. From enough of those it can say things like after news, you usually go to video — 9 of 22 times.

Every number it shows comes with the count behind it, so you can judge whether it has enough evidence to be worth anything. Often it does not, and it says so.

        What you keep control of

        Pause, delete, and forget

        • Pause stops collection immediately and keeps what exists.
        • Raw visits are deleted after 30 days by default, and you can change that to any number of days — or to keeping them forever. What Tise has learned survives, because it is arithmetic, not a copy of your browsing.
        • Delete everything removes every record, your settings, and Tise's permission to read your history. It leaves the extension exactly as it installed: able to store nothing.
        • Export hands you the whole database as a file you can read. It is the same file the research side of this project runs on.

        How to check any of this

        Do not take it on trust

        Tise is open source, and the reasoning behind every decision is published alongside it — including the mistakes and the measurements that failed. If you want to verify the claim on this page rather than believe it: the permissions are in manifest.json, what is stored is the EVENT_FIELDS list, and a test fails the build if this page and that list ever disagree.

        You can stop, pause, or delete everything at any time.

        Tise makes no network requests of any kind. There is no server, no account, no telemetry, and no opt-in that would add one.
